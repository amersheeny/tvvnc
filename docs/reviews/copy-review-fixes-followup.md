STRING: credentialsUnreadable
KEYS: credentialsUnreadable
ENGLISH: "Saved TV settings can no longer be decrypted. To start over, clear TV Console’s storage in phone settings, then add your TVs again. This removes all saved settings and credentials."
VERDICT: REWORD
REWRITE: "Saved TV settings can no longer be decrypted and cannot be recovered. Clear TV Console’s storage in phone settings, then add your TVs again. Clearing storage removes everything this app has saved on this phone."
REASONING: Most of this string is already right and is kept. "Decrypted" is the correct verb rather than jargon smuggled in, because the Settings screen visible in the rendered capture already promises the reader that "Credentials are encrypted on this phone", so this message uses the counterpart of a word the app itself taught them; it also covers both failure causes named in the role, since whether the key died or the stored data failed its authentication check, the honest description of the result is that the data cannot be decrypted. Naming the app as "TV Console" inside the instruction is likewise correct and worth preserving, because the reader has to match that exact name against a list of applications in the phone's own settings, and "in phone settings" matches the wording of the app's existing button for going there. Two things are changed. First, the closing sentence misattributes the loss: "This removes all saved settings and credentials" tells the reader that clearing storage is what will destroy their saved TVs, when the role establishes that the profile metadata is encrypted alongside the credentials and therefore that the names and addresses are already unrecoverable before they touch anything. That misattribution has a cost in behaviour, not just in accuracy — a reader who thinks the data is still there will hesitate, hunt for an export, or look for a way to rescue the list first, and there is nothing to find. The replacement states the permanence up front, where it answers the reader's actual first question, and recasts the final sentence as what clearing storage does reach, which is everything the app holds, without enumerating categories the brief does not establish. Second, "To start over" frames the instruction as one option among several, which invites the reader to try the other thing this app has already recommended to them for a storage problem — restarting the app, as the temporary secure-storage message explicitly advises. In the permanent category that retry is wasted effort, so the instruction is given directly instead. Nothing in the replacement claims the app deleted anything: the removal is attributed to the clearing action the reader performs in phone settings, which is where the role says it must be initiated, and no claim is made about what does or does not invalidate the key.
RISKS: Length and surface. At three sentences this is already beyond what a snackbar can show, so it needs a dialog or a persistent blocking surface; the replacement is about three words longer than the draft and does not change that requirement. Translation note, offered as a consideration rather than a constraint: "TV Console" is the application's own name as it appears in the phone's list of installed apps, so it must be left untranslated or the instruction stops matching what the reader will actually see there.
