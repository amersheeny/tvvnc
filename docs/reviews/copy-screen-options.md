REWRITE: Screen options
REASONING: The noun "controls" is already spoken for in this product, and it points at the television rather than at the phone. The shipped empty-state sentence visible in the supplied portrait capture reads "Screen sharing is unavailable. Try the remote controls.", which teaches the user that "controls" means the things that operate the television, and the earlier screen inventory places genuine television controls one page away under REMOTE, TOUCHPAD and DIRECT TOUCH alongside physical television buttons. On a product whose entire purpose is operating a Sony television from a phone, a button labelled "Screen controls" therefore has a real chance of being read as controls for the television's screen, meaning picture or display settings on the set itself. That is precisely the reading the role statement rules out, so the submitted label invites the one misunderstanding the brief was careful to exclude. Swapping the single loaded word removes that reading without disturbing anything else. "Options" is the collective Android itself uses for a menu gathered behind a button, so the label announces as something that opens a list of choices rather than as something that operates a device, and that shift is exactly the distinction this button needs to make. The scoping word stays "Screen" because that is the product's own noun for this pane, shipped in "Hide screen", "Full screen" and "Screen sharing is unavailable" and carried into the accessibility name "Screen height" already approved for the grip in the same band; keeping it means the two controls in the resize band share a prefix, so a screen-reader user swiping through the band hears "Screen height" and then "Screen options" and correctly understands both as belonging to the pane above. A scoped name rather than a bare "More options" is right here, and the supplied landscape capture at /Users/amsh/worktrees/tvVNC/zoom-runtime/screenshots/keyboard-wide-ime.png is the proof: the app bar in that image already carries a three-dot overflow button at the top right, beside the X, so the screen will hold two menu buttons at once and a generic name on the second would leave a screen-reader user unable to tell them apart. On the fit between the name and the list behind it, the menu mixes view choices with outright actions, since Fit, Actual size, Zoom in and Zoom out are choices while Save screenshot and Reconnect are things that happen; "options" stretches slightly over the actions, but that stretch is invisible in practice, because the platform's own "More options" menus routinely hold Share, Delete and Print. The stretch nobody notices is the right trade against the misreading that sends a user looking for television picture settings.
RISKS: Reconnect is a connection action rather than anything to do with the pane, and it is the one item the name covers least well; if further non-view actions are added to this menu later, "Screen options" stops describing its contents and the label must be revisited rather than quietly stretched further. The label is carrying the whole burden of distinguishing this button from the app-bar overflow, because if the band's button uses the standard three-dot icon the user will see two visually identical three-dot buttons on one screen, one in the app bar at the top right as shown in the landscape capture and one in the band; whether the band's button should use a different icon is an interaction and visual decision outside this review, but it should be made deliberately rather than by default. This menu only exists when the pane is too short to fit both the toolbar and the band, so a user may meet this label once and never again, which is the strongest argument for the plainest available wording and a reason not to trade it later for anything more clever. The supplied landscape capture shows the current uncorrected layout, where the viewer toolbar occupies a full row of eight unlabelled icons and the resize band falls below the visible area behind the phone keyboard; it therefore evidences the problem the correction addresses and is not evidence about the corrected surface, which has never been rendered. The adjacency of "Screen height" and "Screen options" inside one band, and the tooltip's fit on a compact button, must both be checked on the real screen in the post-build review.

BRIEF DEFECT: Two items, both narrow. First, the brief states that the app bar's overflow menu is unchanged but does not supply its exact accessibility name, and that name is the one string this label has to be distinguishable from, since the supplied landscape capture confirms both buttons will sit on the same screen. If the app bar's overflow uses the platform default "More options", the pair is clearly distinct and this label is safe; if it carries some other wording that also begins with a scoping noun, the two need checking side by side before either ships. Second, the brief describes the new button as a "More" button while submitting a fully scoped name for it, which leaves it unclear whether the surface is meant to read as a generic overflow that happens to be named, or as a named menu. That distinction does not change the approved words, but it does drive the icon choice noted above, and the two decisions should be made together rather than separately. Neither item blocks the rewrite. As disclosed throughout, the corrected band and its menu have never been rendered, so every judgement here about adjacency, fit and icon collision is reasoning from the three portrait captures and the one landscape capture supplied, and must be confirmed on the real screen after implementation.
